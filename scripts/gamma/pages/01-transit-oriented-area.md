# Is my house in a Transit-Oriented Area? What Vancouver's TOA tiers mean for homeowners

**Aparna Kapur, REALTOR® · Oakwyn Realty Ltd. · Vancouver, BC · 604-612-7694**

If you own a house within a 10-minute walk of a SkyTrain station in Vancouver, provincial law now sets a minimum density the City must allow on your block. That does not rezone your lot on its own, and it does not put a developer on your doorstep. It does change what your land could be worth to the right buyer, and it is worth understanding before anyone knocks.

---

## What a Transit-Oriented Area is

In late 2023 the Province of British Columbia passed Bill 47, which requires municipalities to designate Transit-Oriented Areas (TOAs) around rapid transit stations and major bus exchanges. Vancouver adopted its Transit-Oriented Areas Designation By-law in June 2024.

Vancouver has 29 TOAs: 27 SkyTrain stations plus two bus exchanges, Dunbar Loop and Kootenay Loop. On the west and south side that includes every Canada Line station from Marine Drive to Olympic Village, and the Broadway Subway stations now under construction such as Oak-VGH, South Granville and Arbutus.

---

## The three tiers

The rules are set by distance from the station entrance, measured as the crow flies.

| Tier   | Distance from a SkyTrain station | Minimum allowable height | Minimum allowable density |
| ------ | -------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Tier 1 | Within 200 m                     | Up to 20 storeys         | Up to 5.5 FSR             |
| Tier 2 | Within 400 m                     | Up to 12 storeys         | Up to 4.0 FSR             |
| Tier 3 | Within 800 m                     | Up to 8 storeys          | Up to 3.0 FSR             |

Bus exchange TOAs (Dunbar Loop, Kootenay Loop) use smaller radii and lower tiers. Off-street parking minimums for residential use have been removed inside every TOA.

Source: City of Vancouver, Transit-Oriented Areas printable guide and Rezoning Policy for Transit-Oriented Areas, 2024.

---

## What it does not do

A TOA designation does not change your zoning. Your R1-1 lot is still R1-1 the day after the by-law. What the by-law says is that if someone applies to rezone your lot for housing, the City cannot approve less than the tier minimum on the grounds of height or density alone.

It also does not override an existing area plan. If your block sits inside the Broadway Plan or the Cambie Corridor Plan, that plan governs the form of development, and in many blocks it already allows more than the TOA minimum.

---

## Why it changes your lot value anyway

A house is priced against other houses. A development site is priced per buildable square foot. Once a block is eligible for 8, 12 or 20 storeys, a developer values the land by what can be built, and the house on it becomes irrelevant.

Three things decide whether that is a real number for your lot:

1. **Site size.** A tower needs an assembled site, often several adjacent lots. A single 33-foot lot in Tier 1 is not a tower site on its own.
2. **Market timing.** Vancouver development slowed sharply through 2025 and 2026 on construction costs and softer presale demand. Some owners who were optioned in 2024 saw those options lapse.
3. **The rest of the block.** Assemblies need willing neighbours. Whether yours are willing is something you find out before you price.

---

## How to check your own address

1. Find your nearest SkyTrain station and measure the straight-line distance on a map. Under 200 m, 400 m or 800 m tells you the tier.
2. Check whether your block is inside the Broadway Plan or Cambie Corridor Plan area. If it is, the plan's sub-area rules apply first.
3. Look up your current zoning on the City of Vancouver's VanMap. R1-1, RT, RM and CD-1 each behave differently.

## Questions homeowners ask

**Will my property taxes go up because I am in a TOA?** BC Assessment values land at highest and best use. If assemblies start trading on your block, assessed values can follow. Vancouver's tax rate is applied to that assessment.

**Do I have to sell?** No. Nothing in Bill 47 or the City by-law compels an owner to sell or redevelop.

**Can I build more on my own lot instead?** On an R1-1 lot you can already build a multiplex of 3 to 6 strata units or up to 8 rental units without rezoning, depending on frontage. Going above that inside a TOA means a rezoning application.
