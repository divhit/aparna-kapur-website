# Broadway Plan: should I sell my house to a developer?

**Aparna Kapur, REALTOR® · Oakwyn Realty Ltd. · Vancouver, BC · 604-612-7694**

If you own a house or duplex between Clark Drive and Vine Street, roughly from 1st Avenue to 16th Avenue, you are inside the Broadway Plan. Since October 2025 much of that area is already zoned for apartment buildings, which means a developer no longer needs a rezoning to build on your block. Here is what that does and does not mean for your price.

---

## What the Broadway Plan is

Vancouver City Council approved the Broadway Plan on June 22, 2022. It covers about 500 blocks around the Broadway Subway line and allows towers and mid-rise buildings across neighbourhoods that were mostly houses and three-storey walk-ups: Kitsilano, Fairview, South Granville and Mount Pleasant.

In December 2024 Council removed the original limit of two towers per block. In October 2025 Council went further and pre-zoned 4,294 parcels across the Broadway Plan and Cambie Corridor Plan areas into three new districts:

| New district | Form                | Height                                      | Density |
| ------------ | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| R3           | Low-rise apartment  | Up to 6 storeys (8 with affordable housing) | 3.0 FSR |
| R4           | Mid-rise apartment  | Around 12 storeys                           | 4.0 FSR |
| R5           | High-rise apartment | Up to 22 storeys                            | 6.5 FSR |

The changes took effect October 21, 2025. An owner or developer in a pre-zoned area can go straight to a development permit, which the City estimates removes 12 to 15 months from the timeline.

Sources: City of Vancouver, "Speeding up housing delivery in Broadway and Cambie Corridors", October 2025; Daily Hive Urbanized, October 2025.

---

## What was pre-zoned and what was not

Not every block got the same treatment. In broad terms:

- **Low-density residential blocks** (houses and duplexes) in the Broadway Plan were pre-zoned.
- **Mixed-use frontages** on Broadway and the arterials such as Arbutus, Burrard, Granville, Main and Fraser were left out and still need a site-specific rezoning.
- **Existing apartment areas** in South Granville, Kitsilano and Mount Pleasant mostly received R3, so a tower on those sites still needs its own rezoning.

The pre-zoning did not loosen the design guidelines, and it did not touch the tenant protection rules. It removed one approval step.

---

## Why a house owner is a different seller from a landlord

The Broadway Plan carries the strongest renter protections in the country. A tenant displaced by redevelopment gets a right of first refusal to return at the lower of their original rent or 20 percent below the CMHC city-wide average, plus moving costs. Those obligations attach to the building, and developers price them in.

If you own and occupy a house, there are no tenants and no relocation obligations. That makes your lot simpler to underwrite than the rental walk-up next door, which is one reason single-family blocks were pre-zoned first.

---

## How developers actually approach you

Most first contact comes from an "assembler", a realtor or consultant working for a developer who is trying to put together enough adjacent lots for a viable site. Watch for three things:

1. **Option agreements with long feasibility periods.** These tie your property up for months while the developer decides. Some owners optioned in 2024 were released in 2025 when the market slowed.
2. **Prices quoted per lot rather than per buildable square foot.** Developers value land by what they can build. If the number you are given does not come with the FSR and the price per buildable foot behind it, you cannot compare it to anything.
3. **Pressure to sign a listing with the assembler.** You are entitled to independent representation. An assembler works for the buyer.

---

## The honest answer on timing

Vancouver development slowed through 2025 and 2026. Construction costs, financing costs and softer presale demand mean fewer projects pencil, and the City's own implementation updates show a large entitlement pipeline that is not yet building. A pre-zoned lot is more valuable than it was, but a sale to a developer depends on a developer who can build now.

That is not a reason to wait or to sell. It is a reason to price the land properly before deciding.
