# What is my Vancouver lot worth to a multiplex builder?

**Aparna Kapur, REALTOR® · Oakwyn Realty Ltd. · Vancouver, BC · 604-612-7694**

Since September 2023, almost every detached lot in Vancouver has been zoned R1-1, which allows a multiplex of up to six strata homes or eight rental homes without a rezoning. Builders have been buying lots for exactly that. Whether yours is one they want, and what they would pay, comes down to a few measurements you can check yourself.

---

## What R1-1 allows

Vancouver City Council approved the multiplex zoning in September 2023, replacing the old RS single-family zones with R1-1 across most of the city. The Province's 2024 small-scale multi-unit housing law (Bill 44) set a June 30, 2026 compliance deadline for municipalities, but R1-1 already allowed more than the law requires, so nothing changed on that date for Vancouver lots.

| Lot frontage              | Maximum units                                         |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| At least 10 m (33 ft)     | 3 to 4 units                                          |
| At least 13.4 m (44 ft)   | Up to 5 units                                         |
| At least 15.1 m (49.5 ft) | Up to 6 strata units, or up to 8 secured rental units |

Base density is 1.0 FSR. There is no minimum off-street parking requirement for multiplexes.

Source: City of Vancouver, multiplexes and R1-1 zoning; Province of BC, Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing guide.

---

## What a builder is actually buying

A builder is not buying your house. They are buying the right to put four, five or six homes on your land and sell them individually. Their maximum price is what those homes will sell for, minus construction, soft costs, financing, City fees and their margin. Everything else is detail.

That means the same street can have two kinds of lot:

- **Lots that pencil.** Wide enough for five or six units, lane access for parking and garbage, flat, no protected trees in the building envelope. These attract builder competition and sell above what a family buyer would pay for the house.
- **Lots that do not.** A 33 ft lot with a big tree in the back yard and no lane is a three-unit site at best, and a builder will pay house value or less.

One independent analysis of Vancouver's R1-1 lots in 2026 concluded that only a small minority pencil for the full six units at current construction costs. Whether or not that figure is right, the point stands: the premium is real for the right lot and absent for the wrong one.

---

## The six things that decide it

1. **Frontage.** 33, 44 and 49.5 ft are the thresholds. Half a metre can change the unit count.
2. **Lane access.** Without a lane, parking and servicing come off the street and the design gets worse.
3. **Corner position.** Corners get light on two sides and often a better layout.
4. **Slope.** A sloping lot adds foundation cost and can add or remove a basement level.
5. **Trees.** Vancouver's Protection of Trees By-law can fix the building footprint before the architect starts.
6. **Area plan.** If your lot is inside the Broadway Plan, the Cambie Corridor Plan or a Transit-Oriented Area tier, R1-1 may not be the highest use at all, and a multiplex builder may be the wrong buyer.

## Sell to a builder, or build it yourself?

Some owners consider building the multiplex and selling the units. It can work, and it carries construction risk, financing cost, two to three years of time, and the BC home flipping tax if a unit is sold within two years of acquisition in some structures. Most owners who ask the question end up choosing between a clean sale to a builder and a clean sale to a family, and the right answer is whichever number is higher after the risk is counted.
