# Oakridge Park and your property value: what nearby homeowners should know

**Aparna Kapur, REALTOR® · Oakwyn Realty Ltd. · Vancouver, BC · 604-612-7694**

Oakridge Park opened its retail on May 28, 2026, and the first residents move into its towers between late 2026 and early 2027. If you own a house or condo in Oakridge, South Cambie, Marpole or along the Cambie Corridor, you are living next to the largest redevelopment in the country. Here is what it changes for your property, split into the two questions owners actually ask.

---

## What is being built

Oakridge Park is the QuadReal and Westbank redevelopment of the former Oakridge Centre at Cambie Street and West 41st Avenue, directly on the Canada Line.

- Around 650,000 sq ft of retail with more than 100 stores, opened May 2026; the Safeway anchor returned in August 2026
- Nearly 1,400 homes across the first five towers expected complete by the end of 2026, a mix of market condominiums and social housing
- At full build-out in 2029: ten towers, more than 4,300 homes including nearly 1,000 secured rental units, a rooftop park, a community centre and a library

Sources: QuadReal, Daily Hive Urbanized and Connect CRE, May to August 2026.

---

## If you own a condo nearby

Two forces pull in opposite directions.

**Supply.** Roughly 1,400 new homes completing in a single neighbourhood over a few months is a lot of competition for resale listings. Presale buyers who cannot or do not want to complete will list assignments and new resales at the same time. In the short term that weighs on prices for comparable one- and two-bedroom units in the area.

**Amenity.** Once the retail, park, community centre and library are open and busy, Oakridge becomes a different address. Buyers pay for that over time, and the older buildings in the neighbourhood benefit from it without having paid for it.

The benchmark apartment price in Oakridge softened over the past year. Whether you should sell into the completion wave or hold through it depends on your unit, your building and your timeline, and it is a conversation worth having with the actual numbers on the table.

## If you own a house nearby

Your question is different. Oakridge-41st Avenue station is a Tier 1 Transit-Oriented Area, which means lots within 200 m are eligible for up to 20 storeys, within 400 m up to 12, and within 800 m up to 8. The Cambie Corridor Plan already set Oakridge as a municipal town centre with the highest densities on the corridor, and in October 2025 the City pre-zoned a group of blocks in the Oakridge off-arterial high-density area so they can go straight to a development permit.

For a single-family lot in that zone, the land can be worth more than the house, but only as part of a site large enough to build on. Most of those sites are assemblies of several neighbouring lots, and the price is set per buildable square foot, not per house.

## What is not changing

- **Your zoning.** Oakridge Park's approvals apply to its own site. Nothing about your lot changed when it opened.
- **Your taxes, immediately.** BC Assessment values land at highest and best use, so assessed values on blocks where assemblies trade can rise. That follows sales; it does not lead them.
- **The school catchments.** Oakridge, Sir William Osler, Jamieson and Eric Hamber catchments were not redrawn by the project, although enrolment pressure is a live question as the towers fill.
