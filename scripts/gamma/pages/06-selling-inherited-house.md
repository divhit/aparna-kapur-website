# Selling an inherited house in Vancouver: probate, taxes and timing

**Aparna Kapur, REALTOR® · Oakwyn Realty Ltd. · Vancouver, BC · 604-612-7694**

If you are the executor of a parent's estate and the largest asset is a house in Vancouver, you are managing a legal process, a tax bill, forty years of belongings and a property sale at the same time, usually while grieving and often from another city. This is the order to do things in, and the mistakes that cost estates the most.

---

## Step one: find out whether you need probate

Probate is the Supreme Court of British Columbia confirming the will and the executor's authority. If the house was owned solely by the person who died, you will need a grant of probate before the Land Title Office will transfer it, and therefore before any sale can complete.

If the house was held in joint tenancy with a surviving spouse, it passes by survivorship and probate is usually not needed for the house.

BC's probate fee is roughly 1.4 percent of the estate's value above $50,000, with a lower rate on the first $50,000 and a court filing fee. On a Vancouver house that is a meaningful number, and it is calculated on the gross value, not net of the mortgage.

Source: Province of British Columbia, Probate Fee Act.

---

## Step two: understand the timeline

- **Grant of probate:** typically two to three months after filing, longer if the registry is busy or the application is returned for corrections
- **210-day waiting period:** under BC's Wills, Estates and Succession Act, an executor generally should not distribute the estate until 210 days after the grant, unless all beneficiaries consent, because of the window for claims against the estate
- **Full settlement:** commonly eight to twelve months, sometimes longer

You can list the house before the grant is issued. You cannot complete the sale until it is. Listing early with a completion date set after the expected grant is common, and buyers in Vancouver are used to it, as long as the contract is drafted for it.

---

## Step three: the taxes, in plain language

**Capital gains.** At death, the deceased is deemed to have sold everything at fair market value. If the house was their principal residence for every year they owned it, the gain is exempt. If it was a rental or a second home for part of that time, there is tax on the estate's final return, and the professional appraisal at date of death sets the number.

**Property transfer tax.** Transfers from an estate to a beneficiary are exempt. When the estate or beneficiary then sells to a buyer, the buyer pays it, as in any sale.

**BC home flipping tax.** BC's tax on properties sold within two years of purchase has an exemption related to death. If a beneficiary takes title and sells quickly, confirm the position with an accountant before listing.

**Vancouver Empty Homes Tax and the Speculation and Vacancy Tax.** Both have exemptions for the year of death and, for the Empty Homes Tax, the following year. The declaration still has to be filed. A missed declaration on an inherited house is one of the most common and most avoidable bills an estate pays.

This is general information. Estates differ, and an accountant or estates lawyer should confirm each of these for yours.

## Step four: decide how to sell

Most West Side houses that come through estates were built between the 1930s and the 1970s and have been lived in for decades. That creates a choice:

- **Sell as-is, land value.** On the west side and south side the land is usually most of the value. Under R1-1 multiplex zoning a builder can put up to six homes on a wide lot, and in the Cambie Corridor or a Transit-Oriented Area the buyer may be a developer assembling a site. These buyers do not care about the kitchen.
- **Sell as a family home.** If the house is sound and the lot is not a development lot, a family buyer will pay for a house they can move into. Light preparation, not renovation, is usually the right amount of work.

Executors often spend money on repairs a builder will demolish. Knowing which buyer you have before spending anything is the single largest saving available to most estates.

## The practical list

1. Get the professional appraisal at date of death; the estate's tax return and the beneficiaries' cost base both depend on it.
2. Keep the insurance in force and tell the insurer the house is vacant. Vacancy clauses void policies.
3. File the Empty Homes Tax and Speculation and Vacancy Tax declarations claiming the estate exemption.
4. Agree among beneficiaries, in writing, who decides on price and terms. One executor with clear authority sells faster and for more than a committee.
5. Clear the house before photographs, not before listing. Junk removal and estate sale companies in Vancouver book weeks ahead.
